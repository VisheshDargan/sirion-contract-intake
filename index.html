Why contract intake

Contract requests are run outside any system. They come in over email, Teams, Slack and walk-ups, usually with details missing. Legal chases the requester for the missing details, then chases every approver one by one. The requester can't see where the request is or who it's waiting on.

Negotiation and the repository both depend on what intake captures. If intake is broken, every later stage starts with gaps.

The solution: Sirion contract intake

Legal runs every supplier contract request from one queue, starting in the Outlook inbox where requests already arrive. Sirion AI reads the request, routes the approvals and keeps the record, so Legal stops chasing and the requester can see where things stand.

Request emailChecked in OutlookIntake queueApprovals by emailDraft ready to negotiate
1

Sirion add-in for Outlook

Legal works from the inbox where requests already arrive.

  • Summarize any email and its thread
  • Check the email and attachments against IBM's intake playbook (8 checks, each linked to its source)
  • Ask questions about the request, with sourced answers
  • Create the request in one click; the requester gets a request number by email
  • Paste any new email and Sirion AI reads it live
2

One intake queue

Every request in one place, whichever channel it came from.

  • Outlook, Teams, Slack, Jira and renewal dates in one Jira-style queue
  • Every status says who the request is waiting on
  • Assign a lawyer by their open work, deadlines and specialism
3

Approval routing

Sirion AI works out who must approve, and why.

  • Rules from the playbook: budget owner, Finance above $100,000, Privacy when personal data is shared
  • One email per approver, drafted with a short brief and one clear ask
  • All approvals go out in parallel, not one after another
4

Approvals by email

Approvers reply from their own inbox. Nobody logs in to Sirion.

  • Sirion AI reads each reply and logs it as an approval, a condition or a change
  • Legal replies and follows up from Sirion; every reply is on record
  • Agreed changes go into the draft, marked with who asked
5

Tracking and reporting

Everyone can see where a request stands, and Legal can see where intake slows down.

  • A journey, a "Waiting on" block and a full history on every request
  • Every request ends in the contract repository with owner, value and renewal date
  • A reporting dashboard: volume, on-time rate and approval time by team

How much Sirion AI does on its own

Sirion AI informs and prepares; people decide and send. Anything that commits IBM, or speaks for Legal, needs a person. A wrong extraction costs a minute to fix. A wrong commitment costs money.

Does on its own

  • Reads every request email and its attachments
  • Checks them against the playbook and flags gaps and risks
  • Sends the requester their request number, and asks for what's missing
  • Logs each approver's reply as an approval, a condition or a change
  • Keeps the summary, status and history up to date

Prepares, a person decides

  • Suggests the approvers and why; Legal removes or adds them
  • Drafts approval requests, replies and reminders; Legal edits and sends
  • Shows each lawyer's workload; legal ops assigns
  • Marks the agreed changes in the draft; the lawyer accepts them

Never does

  • Approve anything on someone's behalf
  • Email a supplier
  • Agree, decline or change contract terms
  • Sign, or mark a contract as signed

If fewer than 70% of the details Sirion AI finds are accepted unchanged, it drops back to suggest-only (see Success metrics).

Success metrics

North-star metric

On-time intake coverage

The share of new supplier contracts that came in through the intake queue and were ready to negotiate within their target time, with every required approval in.

Contracts found later in NetSuite or DocuSign with no request count against it, so going around the process lowers the score. Target times: 2 working days for low risk, 5 for medium, 10 for high. We'd measure the baseline in month one and aim for 80% within two quarters.
